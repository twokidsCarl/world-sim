### 2. Head and face

Measured: MHR's average head (identity 0) is 101 mm from the centre of the eyeball to the top; her skull is 109 mm plus scalp, his 118 mm plus scalp. MHR has no head scale. So when the crown agrees the eyes are high: in the male's original fit (to the outline of his old skin) the eye joints sat 14.5 / 15.0 mm above the centres of his eyeballs; the female target went wrong differently (the head identity was set free and stretched the face to 148 mm from eye to chin).

Both bodies now use the same method:
- Head and neck pose are fitted to the surface "skull + soft-tissue thickness" and to the two eyeball centres; the head identity is not touched (0 for the female, his own for the male). Eye joint to eyeball centre: female 3.9 / 4.2 mm (almost all of it left-right), male 2.5 / 2.7 mm.
- The cranium above the eyes is scaled about the midpoint of the eyes: female x 1.09 / y 1.20 (my upper bound) / z 1.04; male 1.12 / 1.17 / 1.07.
- Below the eyes the height is remapped piecewise through two landmarks: the stomion goes 2 mm above the edge of the upper central incisors, the menton 7 mm below the lowest point of the mandible. Female: stomion 69 -> 63 mm, menton 116 -> 100.5 mm; male: 61 -> 65, 113 -> 105 mm. The female's lower face is also moved back (9.1 mm at the mouth, 4.9 mm at the chin) so that her lips meet the lip surface of the skin; the male needed none (0).
- I did not fit head identity or expression components to facial landmarks: with the identity free (bounded at 2.5) most coefficients ran to their bounds, the RMS did not fall and the result was unusable. What MHR lacks: a scale of the head as a whole and by region, the distance between the eyes, and facial landmarks that can be located (lid margins, mouth corners and so on).

Soft-tissue thicknesses are assumptions (the order of magnitude of the tables used in forensic facial approximation, not checked point by point against a source): vault 6 mm, zygoma 7, nasal bones 4, chin 10, under the menton 7.

### 3. What pushed the eye and mouth regions out; MHR's topology

So your guess was half right: those parts did the pushing, but leaving them out is not enough; the head has to be placed on the eyes.
- Female: before this change there was no enclosure push on lids or lips (max 0.1 mm), but the relaxation stage left up to 9.6 mm of displacement in the lid region; that version was never installed.

Both bodies now: the parts of the eyeball, conjunctiva, lacrimal parts, tarsal plates, lips, teeth, tongue and gingiva, and on each side the ring of orbicularis oculi nearer the centre of the eyeball (chosen by geometry, not by name), are left out of the enclosure set, 78 parts in all, ids listed in manifest.face.notEnclosed.partIds; the vertices of the lid and lip regions get no push at all (two rings of transition at the rim); the push elsewhere on head and face is low-passed. Topology of MHR LOD0: 73,639 vertices in one closed, singly connected surface. Neither the palpebral nor the oral fissure is an opening; there is no separate eyeball (the skin comes no nearer an eye joint than 15.3 mm: one continuous sheet of lid) and no mouth interior (the skin vertices nearest the tongue and teeth joints are 24-54 mm away); the lips meet in a crease at the oral fissure (about 600 vertices are buried in it). So there are no upper or lower lid-margin or lip-margin loops, and I did not cut the mesh. manifest.face.regions gives regions: lidsL / lidsR (about 1070 vertices each) and lips (2049), vertex indices in the order of skin.obj, independent of pose.
